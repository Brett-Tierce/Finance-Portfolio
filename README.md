# Finance-Portfolio
Financial models and analyses built from public company data by Brett Tierce, finance student at Auburn University.

## Alaska Air – Q2 2026 Guidance vs. Actual
Variance analysis of management guidance (Apr 20, 2026) against reported Q2 2026 results (Jul 21, 2026), built in Excel.

Findings: 
- EPS beat guidance (-$0.92 vs. -$1.10), but $0.15 of the $0.18 improvement came from a 42% tax rate on the pretax loss vs. 32% planned, making the beat low quality and not repeatable.
- A strong increase in unit revenues by 8.6% (slightly unfavorable to the 9% midpoint), while capacity growth was up only 1%, meant the majority of growth came from pricing and mix.
- Although strong cost management brought non-fuel operating costs in $32M under forecast and fuel costs came in $30M under forecast, these savings were offset by higher performance-based pay ($35M), higher interest expense from new financing ($5M), and unit revenues slightly below forecast.

## T-Mobile - 3-Statement & DCF Model
Five-year integrated projection of the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement, linked to a DCF valuation, built in Excel.

Findings:
- The DCF model suggests $174.72 per share vs. $154.06 today, using a 7.3% WACC and a 7.0x terminal multiple on a five-year unlevered cash flow forecast.
- Revenue is forecast to compound at 6.1% per year to $118.5bn by FY2030E, with EBITDA margin near 36.0% and net income rising from $11.0bn to $15.3bn.
- Free cash flow after capex of $11.6bn in FY2026E compares with $14.1bn of dividends and buybacks, so shareholder returns are partly debt-funded ($5.0bn of net new debt per year).

## Costco vs. Walmart – R Analysis
Parameterized R Markdown report comparing the risk and return of Costco and Walmart against the S&P 500 over 60 months (Oct 2021 – Sep 2026).

Findings:
- Both stocks outpaced the market: $10,000 grew to $24,471 in Walmart and $21,712 in Costco, well ahead of the S&P 500’s $19,040.
- Walmart had the best risk-adjusted performance, with lower volatility than Costco (20.6% vs. 23.3%) and the highest return (19.6%), resulting in a Sharpe ratio of 0.76 versus the S&P 500’s 0.62 and Costco’s 0.55.
- Walmart also had a lower beta (0.57 vs. 0.88) and market correlation (0.43 vs. 0.59) than Costco, plus an annual alpha of 10.4%, making it the stronger diversifier for a portfolio anchored by an index fund.
- Costco ended 11.1% below its mid-2025 peak and Walmart 18.7% below its 2026 high, while the market finished at a new high, showing how single stocks carry company-specific risks that an index fund spreads out.
